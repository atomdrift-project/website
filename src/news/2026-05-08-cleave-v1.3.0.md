Two big things and a lot of small ones.

**`cleave diff` is the signal.** Supply-chain attacks are, almost by definition, *unexpected change* — a new release that does something the prior one did not. The cheapest way to catch one is to compare the new artifact to the old and ask whether the delta is consistent with the project's normal rate of change. That is what `cleave diff` measures, and as far as we can tell, nobody else in this space is measuring it.

The command takes files or directories and diffs across six scopes — `traits`, `metrics`, `kv`, `symbols`, `strings`, `sections` — with `--scope` to narrow, `--limit-changes` to keep output sane, JSON for tooling, and a colored terminal view for humans. Diffs are order-independent on kv arrays and suppress low-value churn so the signal survives. Because kv now reaches deep into the binary headers (Authenticode, Rich, DWARF, codesign, entitlements, plists), the diff catches tampered provenance, not just behavioral drift.

Each scope also emits an *estimated rate of change* — a normalized score in `[0, 1]` describing how much the artifact moved, weighted by criticality and confidence. This is the part nobody else is doing. A point release that suddenly looks like a major rewrite is a thing you want to know about, and the rate-of-change score is what makes that question answerable at scale. It is also the data stream that will feed *valence*, our forthcoming ML model for ranking releases by supply-chain risk.

![cleave diff against the xz-utils backdoor — clean upstream tarball vs. the compromised release, with the new traits, kv entries, and section changes that gave the implant away, and the rate-of-change score the diff assigns to each scope.](/assets/images/cleave-diff-xzutils.png)

**`cleave kv` got a much bigger menu.** PE, ELF, Mach-O, Office (OLE2 and OOXML), RTF, PDF, PNG/JPEG, JAR and `.class`, `.pyc`, pickle, RPM, and source files. Highlights:

- **Binary provenance.** PE manifests and Authenticode certs/signing data, Rich headers, DWARF producers, Go build info, Mach-O codesign, entitlements, `Info.plist`, and launchd plist data. Most of what you used to need three tools for, in one pass.
- **PDF action/structure.** JavaScript, `Launch`, `URI`, XFA, embedded files, and filter chains, surfaced as kv instead of buried in a tree.
- **Office.** VBA, XLM macros, DDE, external references, template injection, `OLE10Native`, and embedded executables.
- **CHM.** Full support, with LZX decode, member analysis, metrics, and kv.
- **PyInstaller.** Extraction plus kv on the unpacked payload.

![cleave kv on a DPRK Mach-O sample — codesign team, entitlements, Info.plist strings, and launchd persistence keys side by side.](/assets/images/cleave-kv-dprk-macho.png)

**`cleave validate`** grew real fixture validation: hostile, benign, and does-nothing corpora, structured output, validator categories, and `--exclude`. The CI signal is finally about whether cleave is correct, not whether the runner had a bad day.

**Under the hood.** Archive analysis uses the global rayon pool and stops nesting itself into deadlock. ZIP, JAR, CHM, and PyInstaller stay in memory instead of dumping trees to `/tmp`. macOS defaults rayon to performance cores. YARA match storage is capped to what the evidence renderer actually uses. PNG/JPEG skip the pixel decode and keep the structural metrics. Large source files no longer crash tree-sitter on the way in. A few archive and UPX wrappers now inherit their children's findings correctly.
