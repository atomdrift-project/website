---
title: "Atomdrift Scan v2.2.0, cleave v2.2.0, stng v1.7.1, filefacts v1.2.0"
date: 2026-07-06
summary: "Scan v2.2.0 screens with bloom filters, fetches and scans a sample's dependencies by default, and renames its CLI to atomscan — riding on new formats and byte-range evidence spans from cleave, stng, and filefacts underneath."
---

Four releases today, and the headline is that Scan stopped taking a sample at
face value.

**[Atomdrift Scan](/scan/) v2.2.0** puts a bloom filter in front of the model.
Before doing any real work, Scan checks known-good and known-bad sets — and
skips known-good samples by default. Dial the tradeoff with `--mode`: `fast`
(bloom only), `balanced` (the default), or `slow` (always a full scan). The
bigger shift is that interactive scans now **fetch and scan a sample's declared
dependencies and install-command packages by default**, so a compromised
transitive dep can't hide behind a clean-looking manifest; fetched analysis is
cached by content hash and dependency fetch-and-scan runs in parallel, so the
extra reach is cheap on the second pass. The CLI is now `atomscan` (it was
clashing with Avast's `scan`), with `fs`→`path` and `pkg`→`purl` — old names
stay aliased. New `purl` and `url` subcommands scan straight from a package
coordinate (`atomscan purl npm/left-pad@1.3.0`) or a remote URL, `--interpret`
templates now adapt to the sample, and rules and models auto-refresh when they
go stale (`--no-update` to opt out). Thresholds were recalibrated — hostile at
L25, suspicious at L3000 — and a confident-but-opaque ML "hostile" is now held
at suspicious rather than cleared outright. [Release
notes](https://codeberg.org/atomdrift/scan/releases/tag/v2.2.0).

**[cleave](/cleave/) v2.2.0** teaches the decomposer new formats to target —
WASM, Android DEX, CAB, and APK — and adds local reference graphs that link
files back to their package and provenance origins (a new `reference_graph`
type), with registry records grafted into the analysis output. Facts now carry
metric spans: byte ranges pinpointing exactly where each measurement's evidence
lives, so a verdict traces back to the region of the file that produced it. A
new composite `crit: exception` rule sharpens intent signalling for trait
authors. Fixes land where they hurt — YARA cold-bucket and init deadlocks,
base64/XOR false positives in SVGs, and looser IP and metrics validation to cut
noise. [Release
notes](https://codeberg.org/atomdrift/cleave/releases/tag/v2.2.0).

**[stng](/stng/) v1.7.1** gives strings a proper home: a new stng-owned cache
(in-memory plus disk) means every consumer shares one copy instead of
re-extracting, and each string now carries its source byte extent, fixing span
math for UTF-16LE, base64, and stack strings. Stack-string junk — stack fills,
register-save patterns — no longer sneaks past the garbage filter, and a
quadratic XOR-scan slowdown on large payloads is gone (tens of seconds → fast,
identical output). The test suite is 40% quicker, jemalloc bumped to 0.7, and
per-string memory got trimmed. [Release
notes](https://codeberg.org/atomdrift/stng/releases/tag/v1.7.1).

**[filefacts](/filefacts/) v1.2.0** — the parser underneath all of it — adds
WebAssembly, OCI container images, Python sdist, and Android DEX, plus better
npm/gem/oci detection and recognition of SVG, Cypher/CQL, and Python template
files. Windowed entropy metrics now surface a concealed high-entropy region
(peak value + offset) even when a file's overall entropy looks unremarkable,
and every fact carries a byte-range span pointing at its evidence. Registry
metadata gains a `security_hold` flag and a `version_removed` metric. It skips
the XOR seed search on source files with no sign of XOR logic, and the cache
now self-cleans to 90% of capacity instead of aging entries out on a timer.
[Release
notes](https://codeberg.org/atomdrift/filefacts/releases/tag/v1.2.0).
